Session 7 — Curfew · Halloway

Ready to run
Do not over-explain. Let silence sit. No captions. Post, say nothing, wait. Nobody in this is evil.

Prep before anyone logs on

Core concept

A remote town where everyone is indoors by midnight, nine people are missing, and the dead are in the streets. The town is not hiding anything — it has a hole in it. The party will build a documented, coherent case against a dead sheriff and be wrong. The man who did it is the mayor, he is seventy-two, he is helping them, and he does not remember.

Leave the table with: the worst thing in this story was done out of love, and nobody involved is a villain.

Where we left off

Aldric fell at the Seal. The smite transferred to Decker and he spent it. The Seal is quieted, not repaired. They broke into a subway tunnel beneath Meridian, surfaced, and returned to the Lantern.

Carried in: Lucius's journal, the undercity map, the Bow of Bilf the Martyr, the Coin, the Tome, Ford's hound. Full long rest. Decker's patron question unanswered.

Aftermath gets ten minutes. Remains logged, level-up handled, everyone breathes. Then Ezriel slides two call sheets across the desk and the night is about something else.

Above-table flags

  • Scipio's player — the intake-line beat lands in the briefing. Small, no fanfare.
  • Hedy's player — DM him the log mechanic before the session. He shouldn't find it by accident. Hedy is he/him.
  • Bilf's player — he will break curfew. Let him.
  • Everyone — ask each player who their character would go outside for. You need this for The Call.

Tone

Daylight Halloway is genuinely pleasant and you play it that way without irony. The wrongness is in gaps, not atmosphere — sentences that stop, a story with no facts in it, clocks that are all fast.

Do not put dread on the town until midnight.

Never say oblex. Never say rift. Never explain the water. If asked directly what the thing is, the honest in-world answer is that nobody knows — and it should be unsatisfying.

Discord setup

  • #evidence — documents only. You are the only person who posts here. No reactions, no chatter. It has to read like a file, not a conversation.

Why #evidence matters: the thesis is that paper doesn't forget while people do. A Discord channel is a filing cabinet the thing cannot eat. When the party's memory slips in Act 2 and they scroll back, the channel is still right — that's the mechanic running itself, for free.

The central mechanics six systems

1 The Hole — passive memory drain, all session

Everyone in Halloway is being eaten slowly, including the party from the moment they drink anything.

  • No rolls, no tracking, no damage. A fiction mechanic, not a combat one, until Act 3.
  • Roughly once per act, quietly remove one thing the party learned. A name they wrote down that nobody recognises. A detail two players remember differently.
  • Ask an innocent question — who told you that? — and let them fail to answer.
  • Never announce it. If a player pushes, let it slide away.
2 Cassandra's Curse — the aphasia mechanic

Halloway residents want to warn them and cannot. Any time an NPC tries to explain the curfew, the walkers, or the disappearances:

The NPC starts the sentence. Two-thirds of the way through they stop — not interrupted, not deciding against it. Losing it. They blink. They look mildly embarrassed. They pick up somewhere else, or they say sorry, what was I — and the party watches a person's own thought get taken out of their mouth.
  • First time: play it as distraction. Let them miss it.
  • Second time: same NPC, different day. Now it's a pattern.
  • Third onward: somebody notices they're doing it.
"I've been trying to tell you since you got here."

Spend that line once, on the right NPC. Mackey is the right NPC if a player is invested in her.

A PC can help. Insight DC 15 to notice it happening. If a PC writes the sentence down mid-way and hands the NPC the paper, they can finish it in writing. Paper doesn't forget. Reward this heavily — it's the thesis of the session.

Exempt: Cassandra Ardley (fifty years off the water) and Hedy.

3 Hedy's Log — the continuity check

Hedy's organic memory drains like everyone else's. His systems do not.

  • Once per act, when the party's recollection has slipped, he may consult his log as a free action and get the accurate version.
  • Send it in DM. He decides whether to say it out loud — that decision is the mechanic.
  • The first time the log and the table disagree is the discovery moment. Let his player say that's not what happened.
  • Following the discrepancies backward points at intake: everything they've consumed in Halloway came out of a tap.
  • He is not immune to Memory Drain in combat. The log is a record, not a shield.
4 Midnight — the curfew
11:00
Doors shut. Curtains close. Porch lights go off, not on.
11:30
Every window in town is covered. This is the entire defence.
12:00
The walkers are on the streets until first light.

The rule is not "don't be seen." It is "don't look." Nobody says this to the party.

Being outside is not automatically fatal. Being seen and answered is. A walker that fixes on a creature approaches, stops at a distance, and Calls. Walk away and it does not pursue.

5 The Call — DC 15 Wis

When a walker fixes on a creature it says a name — a real one, taken from that creature's own memory. It says it warmly.

  • DC 15 Wisdom save. On a failure the creature must use its movement to approach on its next turn.
  • Repeat at the start of each of its turns. Each subsequent save is at disadvantage while the walker keeps calling.
  • Three consecutive failures and the creature reaches it. Then Memory Drain.
  • An ally breaks it with an action — restraint, blocking line of sight, covering the ears. No roll. Say this out loud early so the counter is discoverable in the first encounter.

The names. Lucius for Scipio. Sorrek for Hedy. The Martyr for Bilf. Ford's is the hound — and the hound is standing right next to him. Send the name in DM, one word.

6 The Records — why paper wins

Anything written down is immune to all of the above. Files, flyers, photographs, the case file, the #evidence channel, and anything a PC writes in their own notes.

This is the party's only reliable instrument and it should feel like one.

Act 1 — Halloway ~40 min

The briefing 10 min · at the Lantern

Ezriel has two call sheets off Unclassified Incident Intake — the crank extension at Meridian PD that routes to Observation. Six weeks apart, nobody connected them but him.

  • Mayor Daniel Crale, Halloway. Nine missing in eighteen months. No county file. Worked up the chain until somebody handed him a number to get rid of him.
  • A night nurse at Cedarline Care. A ninety-five-year-old resident who says the same three things at four in the morning.

Midnight — the collection

first-sighting #play · before they know what it is collection #play · run the challenge over this

Skill challenge first. A neighbour comes out to walk them in. Then a second. Then six, then a deputy. Nobody raises their voice.

  • 3 successes before 3 failures. Persuasion, Insight, Intimidation, Deception. DC 14.
  • Success buys information, not freedom — a name, the fact it started eighteen months ago, that Mrs. someone went out and didn't come back. All fragmentary, all interrupted by the Curse.
  • Failure means hands on them. Not violence — shepherding. Six ordinary people who will physically move a stranger off a street.
  • If they escalate to combat, let them. Commoner stat blocks. They don't fight back well. They keep apologising.

Nobody here is an antagonist. They are terrified people improvising a rule that is eighteen months old, and they cannot say why.

The button — end the act here

While they're walking you back, somewhere down the street a door opens. A screen door — that specific sound, the spring and the slap. Then a voice. Warm, ordinary, unhurried, saying a name you don't know.

The people walking you back do not react. They do not turn around. They keep talking about the weather, and one of them puts a hand on Bilf's back to move him along.
Post an image here. After ten drops, cutting to nothing is the beat. No follow-up, no investigation tonight, no roll.

Cassandra Ardley

Seventy-five. Left Halloway at nineteen, came back at seventy. Fifty years off that water — the only person in town who can say Laura Ardley and mean a person.

She gives them: her sister as a human being, the bullying, the reservoir incident, and the fact that she has been telling people this for five years and they nod and forget. She has stopped expecting to be believed and will be openly surprised when the party writes it down.

The false case — let it feel great

Coherent, documented, and wrong:

  • Emmet had picked Wes Harrow up twice. Everyone knew he couldn't stand the kid.
  • He dragged the reservoir alone, at three in the morning, and reported it clean.
  • He drank afterward. Got strange. Never ran again.
  • There is a quiet local legend that the old sheriff did it.

Do not undercut this. The party should leave Act 2 pleased with themselves. That is what makes the flip land.

The mayor

Over a drink, reluctantly, because they asked and he is tired of carrying it:

"I've always thought my father killed those two kids."

Give him the entire scene. He is credible by every measure available to the table. No tell, no hesitation, no wrong detail. He believes it completely; it is the one fragment the thing left him.

Re-post his portrait. It's pinned. Let them scroll up to it if they want a face.

The thing comes up

Only when the hole starts closing — the truth said out loud, the remains recovered, or Emmet's window opening. Run it at the reservoir; the water is where it has to be fought.

the-thing #play · post once, never again

Beats that make it this fight, in order:

  • It does not attack first. It surfaces and it calls, in nine voices at once, and every one is a name somebody at the table has lost.
  • Laura is in the way. Between them and it, not understanding, asking them to stop.
  • Memory Drain lands. Take something real — a player's note, a name, the case. Make them feel a hole open.
  • Killing it is a choice, not a victory. The moment they commit to lethal damage, tell them plainly what goes with it.
  • It cannot stop them filing. No hands, no reach into paper. If the plan runs through the record it will fail to prevent them, and it will keep trying anyway.

If a player asks whether they recognise any of the faces — yes. Give them a name off the flyer sheet. Marguerite Hollis. Rosa Salas. Everett and Maeve, next to each other.

The Choice

No image. Nothing on screen. Just the question.

Kill it

Everyone it holds stops existing — Halloway's dead, including Laura, who is simultaneously the only surviving witness and the only surviving evidence. Scipio closes a sixty-year-old double homicide by destroying the witness. The town gets its evenings back and loses its family. And it may get its memory back with it — which means learning all nine deaths at once.

Break the bargain

Only the man who made it can unmake it, and he has minutes of clarity a day and not many days left. It means getting Emmet to say it out loud, and it means Danny finding out what he did at seventy-two, from his father, in a hospice room — and then a town learning it about the best man in it. The walkers may or may not be released.

Resolve it for them. Editorialise afterward. Debrief. End on an image.

Cast living and otherwise

Mayor Daniel Crale, 72

The killer · your most cooperative contact

No memory of it. Beloved, effective, decent. Believes his father did it and has quietly grieved and half-hated him for sixty years — the one fragment the thing left him. His whole civic life is penance misfiled against the wrong man. He is the reason they were called. Nine people gone on his watch and he cannot live with it.

Emmet Crale, ~95

The father · the man who signed

Hospice at Cedarline, an hour out on a bad road — off the reservoir, drinking different water, which is why his mind can decay normally and why nobody can manage what comes out of his mouth. He agreed to hold the night so his son wouldn't have to, and has held it alone for sixty years. The last uneaten copy of the truth, dissolving.

Sheriff Ruth Mackey, 50s

Competent · composed · privately fraying

Third holder of the office since Emmet. Enforces a curfew with no ordinance behind it because the job was handed to her that way, twice removed. Not corrupt and not stupid — she is losing people and has nothing. Played attractive and dry-witted; the exhaustion lives under the composure. Spend "I've been trying to tell you" on her. Do not make her one of the taken.

Cassandra Ardley, 75

Laura's sister · the only clear head in town

Left Halloway at nineteen, came back at seventy — fifty years off that water. The only person alive in town who can say Laura's name and mean a person. She remembers the bullying, the reservoir, and her sister. She has been telling people for five years and they nod and forget.

Laura Ardley, 18

The first walker · Deer View Diner

Walking home from a shift, sixty years running. A composite — her own last minutes, Danny's view of her, and sixty years of the town's fond half-memory. Let her get a detail about her own life wrong. She does not know she is dead and cannot hold it past dawn. She can describe her killer perfectly: a twelve-year-old boy.

Wes Harrow, 18

The incomplete walker

The thing only ever had him from the outside. He never speaks, keeps his distance, and the face does not resolve. The party will register the difference between the two walkers long before they understand what it means. He bullied Danny for two years and held him under at the reservoir. He did not deserve this.

The Two Accounts

The thing holds Laura's experience and Danny's intent, and they do not match. From where she stood, she was murdered. From where he stood, he panicked. Neither is a lie and there is no reconciling them. The party cannot get a clean answer because one does not exist.

The coroner arrived at the same place independently: Laura's Manner of Death is blank. Wes's says HOMICIDE.
